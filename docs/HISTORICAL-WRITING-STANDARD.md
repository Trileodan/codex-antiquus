# Codex Antiquus — Historical Writing Standard

The original app is the foundation. Keep its coin collection, globe, diagrams, source labels, reader navigation, chapter depth, and existing history. Improve clarity without reducing scope.

## Reader experience
Write as a knowledgeable, engaging history teacher talking to an intelligent curious learner who may not know specialized vocabulary. The goal is understanding, not sounding scholarly.

1. **Narrative depth:** Preserve substantial lessons. Major battles should explain context, personalities, decisions, geography, tactics, consequences, and uncertainty. Do not replace chapters with 100-word summaries or flashcards.
2. **Explain as you go:** Introduce an unfamiliar word only with an accessible definition or immediate example. For example, 'flank — the side of an army's formation.' Keep accurate terminology so the reader learns it.
3. **Show causality:** Say *why* a decision made sense at the time and *how* one event produced the next. Link local events to the larger historical picture.
4. **Human stakes without invented psychology:** Avoid attributing unrecorded thoughts, exact dialogue or certainty about motives. 'It may have appeared...' is better than 'Varro knew...'.
5. **Source honesty:** Distinguish ancient claims from modern conclusions. Note when ancient casualty figures or speeches are disputed. Preserve classifications and references.
6. **Readable prose:** Prefer short-to-medium sentences with varied rhythm. Avoid inflated language, rhetorical fragments stacked for drama, unexplained Latin, and academic hedging that hides the actual point.
7. **Teaching diagrams:** Keep battle maps and phase diagrams; refer to them at the moment they help explain movement.
8. **Learning and assessment:** Assess the meaning of the history, not only memorized dates. Preserve the Year Drop for cold chronology recall.
9. **Connections:** Explain relevant 'Meanwhile' links across civilizations when they help the reader orient events.
10. **Review:** Check factual claims and dates before publication. Don't claim verification without actually consulting evidence.

## Preservation rules
- Do not redesign or remove the original coin collection or interactive globe.
- Do not erase existing lessons, coin unlocks, bookmarks or saved progress.
- Write and review one substantial section at a time, then test in the existing app.
- Keep the main website unchanged until the revised reader has been reviewed.
